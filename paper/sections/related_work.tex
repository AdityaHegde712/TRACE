\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Diversity and joint error.}
The double-fault measure comes from the classifier-ensemble literature, where
Kuncheva and Whitaker~\cite{kuncheva2003} compared it with other pairwise
diversity measures and with ensemble accuracy. We use it as defined there. Our
change is the domain: agents observing one physical scene rather than
classifiers scored on a shared test set.

\paragraph{Unknown correlation in fusion.}
Covariance intersection~\cite{julier1997} fuses estimates when the cross
correlation between sources is unknown, by choosing a conservative bound that
holds for any correlation. It works on continuous state estimates. We study
discrete detection outcomes and ask how much correlation exists in a concrete
benchmark.

\paragraph{Source dependence in truth discovery.}
Dong et al.~\cite{dong2009} show that data sources that copy each other make
naive voting overconfident, and that detecting the dependence repairs the
vote. Our sources share a scene, not a copying relation, but the failure
mechanism is the same: dependent sources counted as independent.

\paragraph{Estimating shared errors.}
Estimating the rate at which two imperfect observers both fail is a
capture--recapture problem. Both-missed cases leave no trace in the reports,
so the quantity is not identifiable from reports alone~\cite{albert2004}.
This is why we fit the dependence factor offline on labelled scenes and
freeze it before evaluation.

\paragraph{Simulator and detectors.}
We render with CARLA~\cite{dosovitskiy2017}. The detectors are YOLO11m from the
Ultralytics release~\cite{ultralytics2024} and RT-DETR-L~\cite{zhao2024}. Both
are COCO-pretrained, so ``different detector'' here does not mean trained on
disjoint data.
